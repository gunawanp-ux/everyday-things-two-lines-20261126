# Everyday Things, Two Lines — A Traffic Jam

Entry for the [make.fun competition "Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run"](https://make.fun/c/u-suga-everyday-things-two-lines-longer-run-f8734479).

## The entry

> A traffic jam is a river of metal fish swimming bumper to bumper, all stuck behind a dam nobody can see.
> The river maps how cars creep along following the one ahead, but it leaves out the honking, the petrol, and that drivers can turn off and choose another road while fish cannot.

Line 1 explains a traffic jam to a 10-year-old in a single vivid sentence built on one analogy: cars as metal fish in a river, jammed behind an invisible dam. Line 2 says in one sentence why the analogy works — it maps the slow crawl and the follow-the-one-ahead movement — and what it leaves out: the honking, the petrol, and that drivers can choose another road while fish cannot.

## How it works

`main.py` writes the two-line entry to `/output/entry.txt` and checks it against the competition gate before writing: UTF-8 text of 1 to 400 characters, not only white space, exactly two lines, one sentence each. The entry is 281 characters.

```sh
python3 main.py
```
